\subsection{Corroboration: the cost is real processor time}\label{sec:abcorroboration}

An in-situ figure of this magnitude has two readings. Either it is processor
time the service spends, or it is time a request spends waiting --- queued, or
descheduled --- which a latency histogram cannot distinguish from work. We
separate them with an A/B of the same deployment at one arrival rate: one window
with the validation call in the handler, one with it removed, the same images,
the same ingress and the same open-loop driver. Table~\ref{tab:ab} reports it.

Withdrawing the validation call removes \ABDeltaCpuUs~$\mu$s of application CPU
per request, \ABDeltaCpuPct\% of the enabled arm's. That quantity is processor
time by construction: a request that queues or is descheduled accrues wall clock
but no CPU, so a difference this size in CPU cannot be queueing delay. The mean
latency falls by \ABDeltaWall~ms over the same windows, and the near equality of
that figure with the CPU difference is the expected relation if the added time
is spent executing rather than waiting.

Two controls hold while it does. Sidecar CPU moves by
\ABDeltaSideMc{} millicores --- in the opposite direction, and by a small
fraction of the application's change --- so the difference is work in the
application container and not in the mesh. Event-loop lag at the 99th percentile
is \ABEnabledLoopLag~ms against \ABDisabledLoopLag~ms, unchanged within the
resolution of the measurement, so the service is not near saturation in either
arm and the cost is not an artefact of a blocked loop.

The magnitude also reconciles with Section~\ref{sec:rq2} without any fitting.
The deployed image is \texttt{node:18-alpine}, whose discarded parse
Table~\ref{tab:matrix} times at \NodeEighteenProbeThrows~$\mu$s in isolation.
The A/B attributes \ABDeltaCpuUs~$\mu$s of CPU per request to the validation
call in deployment. Two instruments that share no code and no arithmetic ---
a single-process microbenchmark and a container CPU accounting over
\ABReqs{} requests --- land within a few percent of one another.

What this does not establish, we state plainly. The two arms are separate
windows rather than interleaved rounds, so the difference is a difference of two
observations and carries no interval. The host load averages at the two window
starts stand at \ABEnabledLoad{} and \ABDisabledLoad, a ratio of
\ABLoadRatio, and the heavier load falls on the enabled arm; contention inflates
a duration rather than deflating it, so the latency difference is an upper bound
on the effect and the CPU difference, which is far less sensitive to contention,
is the figure to rely on.
